# Respectful Conduct at Sacred Places

## Definition
Visitor etiquette at Balinese sacred places consists of respectful behavior toward religious spaces, worshippers, ceremonies, and local instructions.

## Cultural context
A Denpasar Tourism Office page reproducing the Bali provincial government's 2023 foreign-tourist guidance asks visitors to respect local religion and sacred places, respect traditional ceremonies, dress appropriately, and behave properly in sacred places.

## Background
The cited government page is dated 8 June 2023 and refers to Governor Circular Letter No. 04 of 2023. This entry does not assert that this is the latest applicable circular or the complete current legal position.

## Significance
Temples and sacred spaces are active religious environments. Visitor behavior should prioritize worshippers and the instructions of the temple or local authority.

## Common situations
- Entering a temple as a visitor
- Encountering an ongoing ceremony
- Taking photographs near worshippers or sacred objects

## Do
- Follow signs and instructions from temple staff or local guides
- Dress modestly and use required temple attire where applicable
- Ask permission before photographing people, especially during ceremonies
- Keep a respectful distance and avoid disrupting worship

## Don't
- Assume every temple has identical visitor access rules
- Enter restricted areas without permission
- Climb or pose disrespectfully on sacred structures or objects

## Misconceptions
- A temple should not be treated simply as a tourist monument; it is also a place of worship.

## Regional variations
- Access rules, dress requirements, photography rules, and ceremony restrictions can vary by temple and occasion.

## Notes
When asked about a specific temple, the assistant should defer to current signage, temple staff, and the relevant local authority. Current regulations must be checked separately before presenting legal advice.
