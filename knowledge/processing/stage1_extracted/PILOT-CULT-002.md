# Canang Sari

## Definition
Canang Sari is an offering used in daily worship by Balinese Hindus. A local government cultural article describes it as a small but essential element in offerings and yadnya.

## Cultural context
The Buleleng Culture and Tourism Office article explains Canang Sari as an expression associated with prayer and a request for Widya (knowledge) before Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa. It also describes symbolic elements such as the ceper and porosan.

## Background
The same article attributes its creation to Mpu Sangkulputih. This attribution is recorded here as the account presented by that source, not as an independently established historical conclusion.

## Significance
It is part of everyday devotional practice and should not be treated merely as decoration or litter.

## Common situations
- Offerings placed near homes, shops, shrines, or other locations
- Visitors encountering offerings on the ground or near entrances

## Do
- Notice offerings and walk around them where practical
- Ask a local host or guide if unsure what an offering is or where it has been placed

## Don't
- Deliberately step on, move, kick, or disturb an offering
- Assume every offering has the same form or meaning

## Misconceptions
- Canang Sari is not simply decorative material; the cited local-government source places it within daily worship.

## Regional variations
- Forms, components, and explanations may vary with the type of ceremony, offering, and local practice.

## Notes
The source article itself points readers to a secondary blog for further detail. This entry therefore limits its claims to the information stated on the government page and flags detailed symbolism for further expert review.
