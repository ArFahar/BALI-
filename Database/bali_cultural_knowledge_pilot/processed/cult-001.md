# Tri Hita Karana

- **ID:** CULT-001
- **Category:** cultural_fundamentals / balinese_philosophy
- **Scope:** general
- **Verification:** partially_verified (high)
- **Local review required:** True

## Definition
Tri Hita Karana is a Balinese philosophical framework commonly explained through three relationships that support well-being and harmony: Parahyangan (relationship between humans and the divine), Pawongan (relationships among people), and Palemahan (relationship between humans and the environment).

## Cultural context
The framework is used in discussions of Balinese social, spiritual, and environmental life. Government planning materials and cultural publications describe the three dimensions as interconnected rather than isolated categories.

## Significance
It provides a useful introductory lens for understanding why spiritual spaces, community relationships, and care for the environment can be connected in Balinese cultural contexts.

## Common situations
- Seeing a temple or shrine embedded in a community setting
- Learning about customary village life
- Encountering cultural explanations related to environmental stewardship

## Visitor guidance

### Do
- Treat the three dimensions as a broad interpretive framework, not as a complete explanation of every local practice
- Ask local guides or community representatives how the concept is understood in the specific place you are visiting

### Don't
- Assume every Balinese person or village interprets or applies the concept identically
- Reduce the concept to a simple tourist slogan or a rigid three-part checklist

## Misconceptions
- Tri Hita Karana is not simply a zoning diagram; it describes relationships that can overlap in practice.

## Regional variations
- Its application and emphasis may differ across institutions, villages, and contexts.

## Additional notes
For public-facing answers, explain the three terms in plain language and avoid presenting one interpretation as exhaustive.

## Verification notes
Core three-part explanation is supported by provincial and MDA sources. Interpretation and lived application should be reviewed with a Balinese cultural practitioner.
